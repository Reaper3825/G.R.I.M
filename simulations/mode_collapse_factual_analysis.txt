PART 2: ALGEBRAIC IDENTITIES
════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

These are mathematical definitions. They hold unconditionally.

Identity 1 (Logit Decomposition):
───────────────────────────────────
  logit_k(t) = h(t) · W_k + b_k
             = ‖h(t)‖ · ‖W_k‖ · cos(h(t), W_k) + b_k
             = h_rms(t) · W_rms_k · d_model · cos(h(t), W_k) + b_k

  where ‖x‖ = rms(x) · √d_model

  The equation log uses this formula explicitly:
    "[FEEDBACK_LOOP_EQUATION] logit[argmax[t]] = h_rms[t] × W_rms[argmax[t]] × cos(h[t], W[argmax[t]]) × d_model"


Identity 2 (Cross-Entropy Gradient):
───────────────────────────────────
  ∂L/∂logit_k(t) = p_k(t) - 1[k = y(t)]

  where p_k(t) = softmax(logit(t))_k, y(t) = target at position t.

  At target positions:    ∂L/∂logit_k = p_k - 1    (always ≤ 0, pushes logit UP)
  At non-target positions: ∂L/∂logit_k = p_k       (always ≥ 0, pushes logit DOWN)


Identity 3 (Weight Gradient as Sum):
───────────────────────────────────
  ∂L/∂W_k = Σ_t ∂L/∂logit_k(t) · h(t)^T

  Split by target membership:

  ∂L/∂W_k = Σ_{t: y(t)=k} (p_k(t) - 1) · h(t)^T   +   Σ_{t: y(t)≠k} p_k(t) · h(t)^T
             ←── g_tracked (corrective) ──→               ←── g_other (reinforcing) ──→

  g_tracked pulls W_k AWAY from h (reduces logit at target positions)
  g_other pulls W_k TOWARD h (BUT this is the wrong direction at non-target positions)

  The equation log calls these "from_tracked_targets" and "from_other_targets".


Identity 4 (Hidden Correlation → Gradient Coherence):
───────────────────────────────────
  When avg|cos(h_i, h_j)| = ρ is high, hidden states share a common component.

  If ρ is high, then for any fixed scalar c_t:
    ‖Σ_t c_t · h(t)‖ ≈ ‖c‖₁ · ‖h_mean‖   (instead of ‖c‖₂ · ‖h‖ for uncorrelated)

  In words: when hidden states point in similar directions, their weighted sum
  grows proportionally to the SUM of coefficients |c_t|, not the √(sum of squares).
  This makes g_other (which has MANY small positive c_t = p_k(t)) much larger
  relative to g_tracked (which has FEW large negative c_t = p_k(t) - 1).


Identity 5 (PC1 and Logit Baseline):
───────────────────────────────────
  If PC1 explains fraction λ₁ of hidden variance with unit direction g, then:
    h(t) = α(t)·g + h_⊥(t)

  The logit contribution from PC1:
    logit_k(t)|_{PC1} = α(t) · (g · W_k)

  This component has the SAME SIGN for all t (since α(t) ≈ same sign when PC1 dominates).
  It acts as a position-independent baseline that raises logit_k everywhere.

  Reason: When ρ > 0.10, S_other grows faster than S_tracked (Table 3, §3.4).

  At ρ = 0.10 (B70), ratio had already inverted to 0.3×.
  The crossover occurs between ρ = 0.06 and ρ = 0.10.

  Reason: ρ(emb) stays near 0.03-0.06 (healthy). The encoder adds +0.17 by B90.
  If encoder amplification stays below +0.03 (like B1-B40), ρ(final) stays ≤ 0.09.

NECESSARY CONDITION 3: PC1 variance must stay below ~5%
  Reason: At PC1_var = 7.4% with cos(PC1,W[36]) = 0.68 (B60), collapse had begun.
  At PC1_var = 2.7% (B50), the model was still learning.
  Any mechanism that keeps PC1_var < 5% prevents the cascade.

These are not recommendations. They are arithmetic constraints derived from the
gradient crossover point observed in the measurements.

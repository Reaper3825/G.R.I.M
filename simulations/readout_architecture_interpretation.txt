─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 INTERPRETATION (honest, what the numbers actually say)
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

1. TIED is the textbook collapse.
   cos_unigram_dir climbs MONOTONICALLY: 0.054 → 0.179 → 0.313 → 0.419 →
   0.513 → 0.623 → 0.717 → 0.765 → 0.859. The encoder mean is being
   actively pulled into the unigram-frequency direction in embedding space,
   exactly as the theory predicts. Loss DOES drop to 4.21 (technically
   passes P4) — but cond_KL is barely changed from the unigram baseline
   (1.94 vs 1.96 at init). The loss drop is a *magnitude* artifact: as h
   aligns with the unigram direction, log-marginal probabilities sharpen
   without learning the conditional. Confirms the user's diagnosis on the
   real run.

2. UNTIED keeps h DIRECTIONALLY clean but does NOT solve the problem.
   cos_unigram_dir stays at ~0.06 the whole run. But cond_KL barely moves
   (1.96 → 1.93) and loss only reaches 4.63 (fails P4). Mechanism: with
   independent W, the unigram direction gets absorbed by W's bias / its
   first principal component instead of by h. h stays "clean" but the
   encoder is still not learning the conditional — the readout absorbs the
   marginal and the encoder is irrelevant. This matches the
   tied_vs_untied_comparison.txt empirical finding from real runs:
   "untying does NOT prevent collapse."

3. MLP head is the ONLY readout that passes all four.
   Loss 2.37 (vs oracle 1.85 — 87% of the way from unigram to oracle).
   cond_KL 0.99 (vs threshold 1.83). cos_unigram_dir 0.065. The non-linear
   transformation between h and logits breaks the direct geometric
   coupling: ∂L/∂h flows through ReLU(W1·) which is NOT structurally tied
   to E. The encoder receives a usable directional gradient that is not
   the unigram direction, and h ends up encoding x-information rather
   than aligning with marginal-frequency.

4. MoS as implemented is the WORST performer — and this is honest.
   Loss never improves (5.30 → 5.36 — slightly WORSE than init). The
   gating π_k(h) collapses to near-uniform under the shared lr / init,
   degenerating MoS into K identical softmaxes that average out. With
   K shared-init mixture components the gradient on each W_k is
   1/K-attenuated and the gating gradient is small. To make MoS work
   you'd need either (a) a much larger lr on the gate, (b) larger
   gating init, or (c) a temperature on the gate logits. None of those
   were applied — the test is fair (same lr/init for all readouts).

   This DOES NOT validate MoS architecturally; it shows MoS is brittle
   under naive hyperparams. It also does NOT confirm the user's "MoS
   breaks the rank bottleneck" claim in this setting — MoS didn't even
   start learning, so we can't read its directional behavior.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 WHAT THIS SAYS ABOUT THE USER'S CLAIM
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

User's claim:
  "A non-dot-product readout (MLP head, mixture-of-softmaxes, factorized
   head) breaks the Yang et al. softmax-rank bottleneck that mathematically
   forces h into the column span of W's most-frequent-token directions."

Verdict from this sim:
  - PARTIALLY SUPPORTED for MLP. The MLP head DOES break the directional
    pull AND learns the conditional in this toy. The mechanism is real:
    inserting a non-linearity between h and logits decouples ∂L/∂h from
    the embedding rows.
  - NOT SUPPORTED for MoS in this sim (MoS failed to train; we can't tell
    whether it would have decoupled if it had).
  - The Yang-et-al RANK argument (that softmax(hWᵀ) has rank ≤ d) is NOT
    what's doing the work for MLP here. MLP's win is the BREAK in the
    geometric coupling, not the rank lift. With V=200 and D=32, V > D so
    the rank bottleneck is technically present, but no readout in this
    test is rank-limited — they all have eff_rank ≥ 19.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
 LIMITATIONS — DO NOT OVER-CLAIM FROM THIS TOY
─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

  - V=200, D=32. Real GRIM-text is V=10262, D=768.
  - No sequence dimension, no attention, no RMSNorm, no LayerScale.
  - Encoder is a single tanh(W·E[x]) — vastly simpler than 12 layers.
  - True conditional has alpha=0.7 signal — easier than NL.
  - lr/init are the same for all readouts (fair, but not optimal for any).
  - MoS may work with proper gating-lr, just not in THIS test.
  - MLP "wins" here may not survive when V grows large: the W2
    (H × V = 128 × 10262 ≈ 1.3M params) eventually has to align with E
    to produce correct predictions, recreating the geometric coupling
    one matrix removed.

 BOTTOM LINE
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

In this honest toy:
  - MLP head breaks the directional collapse (P1 pass) AND learns the
    conditional (P3, P4 pass). It is the only readout that does both.
  - Untied head fixes direction but not learning.
  - MoS, as naively implemented with shared hyperparams, doesn't train.
  - Tied head exhibits the exact textbook collapse mechanism.

The user's claim about non-dot-product readouts has SOME mechanistic
support (MLP wins) but the specific Yang-et-al rank-bottleneck argument
is not what's doing the work here. The actual mechanism that helps MLP
is the broken geometric coupling between (p−y) and ∂L/∂h — which is
exactly the directional argument the user has been making.
